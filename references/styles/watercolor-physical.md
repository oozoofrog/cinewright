# Watercolor physical, v1 (experimental)

`modules/fx2d/watercolor.mjs` exports `validateInput`, `createSolver`, `bakeWatercolor`, `validateBake`, `decodeWatercolor`. `createSolver(config).advance()` processes the next integer frame's events and fixed steps, and returns an independent snapshot. Bake stores every frame and decoder returns the frozen pigment arrays in O(1), without solver state or source events. Treat returned arrays as read-only. `prepare`, `validateScene`, and `collectAssets` are exported by `modules/styles/watercolor-physical/index.mjs`. Scene requires v1, style, even size, fps, frames, and an `assets/*.json` bake. `node scripts/watercolor-bake.mjs config.json new-bake.json` refuses to replace any destination; writes a temporary sibling and hard-links only after complete serialization. SIGINT/SIGTERM terminate the worker and remove its temporary file. Disk must support hard links. `node scripts/watercolor-examples.mjs` builds local public-compositor examples with an ownership ledger.

## Model

A rectangular cell holds surface water W, mobile pigment M per channel, and deposited pigment P. Units are arbitrary amount/cell, dt seconds, diffusion coefficients cell²/second (unit cell spacing), and absorption, evaporation, deposition rates inverse seconds. Each undirected adjacent pair has closed boundaries: water flux `dt*Dw*(Wa-Wb)` and pigment flux `dt*Dp*(Ma-Mb) + waterFlux*upwind(M/W)`; zero water gives zero concentration. Transfer is equal and opposite. Per step, water is removed by `dt*absorption*W` and `dt*evaporation*W`, recorded separately. Mobile pigment deposits `min(M, dt*deposition*(1+min(1,dt*absorption))*M)`; no pigment is lost. Uniform circular events add specified total water/pigment equally to covered cells before steps. Initial state is dry/empty. `4*dt*(Dw+Dp)<=1`, `dt*(absorption+evaporation)<=1`, and `dt*deposition<=1` bound explicit losses/outflow; no substep adaptation. Conservation is subject to floating point rounding. Seed is recorded for forward compatibility but v1 has no stochastic operation. This is a simplified conservative diffusion and advective transport model, not a Navier–Stokes or calibrated paper simulation. No water storage in paper, rewetting, backruns, capillarity, pigment lift, anisotropy, or spectral optics. RGB alpha compositing is order dependent. No claim of physical calibration, 관객 수용, actual audience responses, or user approval.

Memory budget is checked against `width*height*frames*channels*8` (default 64 MiB, ceiling 256 MiB), **not** JSON size, JavaScript object overhead, peak serialization, or decoded heap; deployment needs separate process/file size limits. All frames are held in memory. Validation traverses the complete asset at prepare time. Each frame is drawn as colored grid rectangles, and resolution is visible. Decoder validation is structural, not cryptographic authenticity. Zero/very small circles between cell centers fail during bake.

The pre-existing `wash.mjs` draws a fixed polygon fill and edge; it has no transport or bake cost. In these examples channel 0 support above 0.0001 rises at frames 0/48/96/191 from 0/262/523/820 cells for wet and 101/647/837/904 for overlap (1296 cells total). A fixed polygon's support does not expand by simulation; this is not a matched visual or performance benchmark. Bake JSON is 5,261,424 and 9,331,385 bytes respectively, versus no bake asset for the old polygon. Full render including encoding took approximately 5.2 and 5.9 seconds on this machine; those are not isolated draw timings.

## Sources and reuse

Existing `wash.mjs` is repository code, inspected as the comparison baseline. No external implementation or assets were incorporated.

The solver computes advective pigment as `(flux/water)*pigment`, avoiding overflow for subnormal water and ordinary pigment. Tiny flux may underflow and halt movement; this is a floating-point limit, not a calibrated physical threshold. Conservation tests allow 1e-9 absolute error, including the limiting `4*dt*Dw=1` case and dilute water. Changes at roundoff scale in rebaked inputs were compared against the frozen original compositor for all 192 frames of each demo: every decoded canvas pixel matched.

The overlay calls line-wash `prepare(api,{scene,transparentPaper:true})`. Default line-wash paper drawing remains unchanged. Watercolor calls this option for its overlay; future line drawing order is independent of paper suppression.


## Phase02 independent-review corrections — solver grid-transport-2

Version 2 stops pair water flux and carried pigment when both cells have less than 2^-1022 water (Float64's smallest normal number). Independent pigment diffusion and deposition continue. This explicit dry/subnormal transport policy avoids dividing quantized subnormal flux by water; it does not discard pigment. Loss update computes remaining water first, then splits the actual removed amount into absorption/evaporation, so separately rounded subnormal sinks cannot remove more than available water. The exact accepted 60-frame drying trajectory from independent review and a subnormal two-sink fixture are regression-tested with full water/pigment ledgers. Normal-grid equations and stability bounds remain the same. New bakes identify `grid-transport-2`; decoder accepts completed v1 and v2 bakes because stored pigment frame layout is unchanged. Old frozen v1 assets remain unchanged and usable.

For strict direct-layer Canvas image readback/seek comparisons, create the context with an explicit fixed `{willReadFrequently:true}` or `{willReadFrequently:false}` policy before any context is acquired. Default browser-selected mode can switch rendering backends after repeated readbacks and differ by rounding even within one browser. The compositor explicitly selects false for output/scratch contexts. Direct caller must reset transforms/composition between draws. Pixel identity evidence is bounded to the tested context policy/browser/GPU; numeric bake identity is separate from cross-browser/OS/GPU pixel identity.

## Final solver grid-transport-3 boundary correction

The second independent review found normal donors just above the subnormal cutoff could still lose one quantum more than available after four rounded edge transfers. Version 3 keeps a per-cell donor budget copied from old water and a separate incoming-credit array. Each pair uses `amount=min(abs(flux),donorBudget)`, subtracts exactly that represented amount from its nonnegative budget, credits the neighbor, and carries pigment using the same bounded signed amount. New water is remaining budget plus incoming credits; no `old+negative summed delta` cancellation or deletion of negative material is used. Pair ordering remains fixed. This adds one Float64 grid to transport scratch memory (at most 512 KiB at 256×256). Strict positive water and pairwise transfer accounting are checked around the cutoff across 320 fixtures.

Sink splitting forms `absorption/(absorption+evaporation)` before multiplying the actual removed water. A zero coefficient therefore keeps its ledger exactly zero even at subnormal amounts. Ninety probes cover both disabled-sink directions and the exact independent fixture. New bakes identify `grid-transport-3`; existing completed v1/v2 bakes still validate and decode with their stored pigment frames. Normal-grid results can differ at floating-point rounding precision, so current demonstrations are freshly baked and encoded under v3, rather than relabelling v2 assets. Versioned source and bake hashes distinguish all three models. The two final P3 fixes were made and reverified by the coordinator; the second independent report remains the historical pre-fix verdict.
